Portra spectral product calibration — 2026-11-01

Scope and decision

Keep the pinned Spektrafilm-derived deterministic density/print core and the
existing input-color balance. Calibrate its product rendering instead of changing
film exposure, rebaking the core tables, or treating its LUT transport maximum as
a physical grain calibration. Other presets keep their rendering.

The previous migration was an architectural improvement, but making that response
the default without a wider test pass was premature: two fixed portrait crops
were darker, and the transferred empirical grain looked too strong.

Iteration

1. Sweep 12 pairs of display tone parameters over fixed, documented portrait ROIs.
   Numeric closeness to the older product is a readability aid, not the objective
   of copying its color or a measurement of physical film accuracy. Inspect the
   complete portraits plus held-out backlight, interior, landscape, flowers and
   neon scenes. Select lift 0.65 / ceiling 0.60 to open shadows with a restrained
   midtone change. A stronger candidate can match one ROI more closely but raises
   more of the scene; the final choice includes visual judgment.
2. The first candidate raised the model's printed black along with other shadows.
   Add a smooth source-luma gate over [0, 0.12] so black input keeps its existing
   printed black/fog. This retains the deepest toe. Output luma >= 0.60 stays
   unchanged. Validate grayscale ordering rather than assume it from the formula.
3. Sweep density-grain amplitude 0.45 / 0.65 / 0.80 / 1.00 with fixed seed and
   unchanged optics on portraits and neon. The selected scale is 0.65; it reduces
   distracting texture while retaining grain. The result is empirical density
   noise, not a measured particle model or a complete Spektrafilm spatial renderer.

Rendering contract

Let sourceY and Y be encoded-sRGB luma before and after the spectral print/color
balance stages; x = clamp(Y / ceiling, 0, 1), toe = smoothstep(0, 0.12, sourceY).
Delta = lift * toe * Y * (1-x)^2.
RGB' = RGB + (1-RGB) * Delta / max(1-Y, 1e-6).

This curve is C1 continuous, preserves RGB opponent direction, stays inside gamut
without hard channel clipping, and becomes identity above the ceiling. For a
fixed source gate, dY'/dY >= 1-lift/3. The gray-ramp test also checks the combined
source/model response; this does not imply arbitrary unrelated colors have a
single globally ordered luminance response. There is no semantic face detection,
auto exposure or per-photo setting. The same parameters process all scenes.

Calibration parameters are optional and serialized into material recipes.
Absent means frozen previous behavior; schema-1 rejects this new calibration.
The density data model remains schema 2 because transport/table semantics did
not change. The new preset cache suffix is #spek-material-v2; the old spectral
key resolves explicitly to loadSpectralPortraV1. Legacy MIT model/color-balance
keys retain their separate loaders. User curves remain after this calibration.
Preview/CI/still/Live use the same Metal helper inside the existing finish pass.
No additional textures, full-image passes, table building or disk IO per frame.

Validation method

Focused XCTest covers optional decoding, frozen round trip and malformed bounds;
4,097-step gray ordering, printed black and high-luma identity; 4,913 RGB lattice
points for gamut/opponent direction; grain mean/variance at two scales, three
grays and two seeds; Metal/still/Live parity with WB and curves; DNG orientation,
video encoding, journal integrity, table reuse and cache budgets.

Native batch: 8 public developed-SDR photos + one synthetic control image,
fixed seed 123, four variants (older MIT, first spectral balanced, pure spectral
core, current calibrated) x color/full modes = 72 PNG. Compare the three frozen
variants against the existing spectral-core/native set (at most 1 code value
from floating-point/8-bit quantization). Independently predict the new tone from
its formula and compare native color PNG (<= 2/255); high-luma pixels must stay
within 1/255 of the previous spectral response. Numeric/crop gates are in
scripts/validate_spectral_calibration.py. Keep first-iteration exports under
spectral-calibration/iterations/one-native rather than replacing old evidence.

A first full test pass exposed three RGB-refinement tests sampling Portra's new
identity carrier as if it were the final image. Restore their actual unit boundary
by explicitly applying FilmLookRefinement to the original RGB cube. The existing
Python fixture values and tolerances are unchanged. Make the RGB scene-parity test
exercise that original refinement too, rather than trivially passing identity.
The material path retains separate full-render tests; no failing check was removed.

Reproduction

Use XcodeBuildMCP test_sim with -parallel-testing-enabled NO. For photographic
exports select FilmSpectralMaterialTests and set JUSTSHOOT_SCENE_FIXTURES to the
research root's inputs/, JUSTSHOOT_SPECTRAL_EXPORT to spectral-calibration/native.
For grain candidates select FilmMaterialCalibrationTests and also set
JUSTSHOOT_GRAIN_REVIEW to spectral-calibration/grain. Do not pass this fixture
directory to FilmSceneParityTests: its optional photo names use another contract.
Run the complete scheme without photo environment variables for full regression.

Then use the NumPy/Pillow research environment:
python scripts/validate_spectral_calibration.py --research-root <comparison-root>
The script fails on frozen-render, predicted-tone, highlight or ROI regressions,
writes metrics.json/sweep.json and renders boards and the local review page.

Limits

The ROI EV values describe mean linear-sRGB luminance in a fixed crop of rendered
SDR images. They are not camera exposure corrections. The numeric suite validates
implementation and regression, not universal aesthetic superiority. Physical-film
scan truth, camera/Photos hardware behavior, iOS 17 device compatibility and device
latency/thermal measurements remain outside these simulator results. Spatial DIR
and particle-statistical grain remain separate unimplemented research stages.
